#Software Design Document for the Gaming Room.

The Gaming Room client is focused on expanding the reach of their game, Draw It or Lose It. This game is a team-based drawing and guessing game where players compete to guess what is being drawn. The client's primary goal is to expand the game's accessibility and make it available on several platforms, including browsers and mobile, while ensuring that the system supports multiple teams, players, enforces unique naming conventions, stays within budget, and maintains a single instance of the game in memory at a time.

What I personally did well in this documentation was understanding what goes into developing a game as well as a server. Knowing UML diagrams and outlining the singleton design pattern was a great way to understand class relationships, system architecture, and software requirements. I also developed more knowledge of what a client looks for in terms of practical solutions for server-side hosting, memory management, security, and even possibly a headless server.

Some helpful aspects of working through this design document were organizing my thoughts and applying what I already know about computers, systems, and my own personal server. Breaking down the requirements, as well as understanding the key aspects needed for designing code, especially at the development phase, is what really helped me work through the design document better.

Some areas of improvement for me personally would have been better diagrams or a possible deeper and simplified explanation of how components interact or even the system's physical components. I can envision visual representations being great for audiences and better for me to understand how system architecture works and how client-server interactions function.

I definitely interpreted the user's needs in my own way and applied my knowledge to this documentation. I analyzed their Android implementation and determined how to apply the proper functionalities into a web-based solution. I also understood and appreciated the singleton pattern in GameService to ensure no duplicate names existed and met their standard of considering user needs, ensuring the software is functional and aligned with the client's vision for a better user experience.

My software design approach included several key aspects. I personally wanted to focus on the end user and prioritized the user's needs, ensuring the software is proper for both the user and the client. I also ensured that assessing the pros and cons of different systems and architectures gave the client the best options and a clear understanding of what would suit them best. As for looking ahead and further refining my strategies, I plan on conducting a more comprehensive analysis of what the client seeks and would like to further develop my domain modeling and use of UML diagrams to map out entities and their relationships, as fully understanding how entities and their relationships coincide is vital.
